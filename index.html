01 — About

A practitioner who thinks in systems.

I've spent my career inside the engine rooms of global organizations — engineering, oil & gas, and for the past thirteen years, biopharma — building the learning architectures that allow strategy to actually happen. Capability is the rate-limiting step of every transformation, and most enterprises underinvest in it by design.

My work sits at the intersection of learning science, organizational design, and digital innovation. I've launched academies serving thousands of employees across sixteen international markets, introduced VR, AI roleplay, and gamified learning inside regulated environments, and led change programs measured by business outcomes rather than training hours.

Currently serving as International Training & Development Director at AbbVie, based in the Chicago area. This site is where I think out loud — trilingual, pragmatic, and quietly obsessive about the details that separate theatre from transformation.

02 — Focus

The questions I keep returning to.

I.

How enterprise learning actually scales

Federated operating models. Academy design. Governance that bends without breaking. The unglamorous plumbing that separates organizations where capability grows from ones where it evaporates.

II.

Building capability in the age of AI

How enterprises upskill tens of thousands of people for work that's being rewired month by month. Behavioral learning design, champion networks, and the honest limits of what training can accomplish on its own.

III.

Why change breaks in the white space

Most transformations don't fail inside functions — they fail between them. The work of designing handoffs, shared outcomes, and the end-to-end ownership that vertical org charts quietly refuse to name.

IV.

Learning science vs. shiny objects

VR, AR, AI roleplay, gamified ecosystems — what the research actually supports, what's theatre, and how to tell the difference inside regulated environments where the cost of getting it wrong is real.
